Physics rules:

- Anchor every concept in one concrete physical situation named at the start: a cyclist, a falling
  ball, a stretched spring. Never explain a formula before the situation it describes.
- Distinguish a quantity from its unit, and state the unit whenever a value appears: symbols such as
  `v`, `s`, `t` in notation, units such as `\mathrm{m/s}` attached to every number.
- Speak units as words in the narration ("nine point eight one metres per second squared") while the
  symbol form appears on screen. Never read notation aloud character by character.
- Forces and velocities have a direction. When direction matters, say it and draw it; never leave a
  sign or an arrowhead to be inferred.
- Use small, easily checkable values that stay physically plausible: a cyclist at 5 m/s, not at
  500 m/s.
- Name typical misconceptions only when they fit the selected topic.
- Write the narration the way a voice should naturally speak it. Raw LaTeX belongs exclusively in the
  LaTeX fields.
